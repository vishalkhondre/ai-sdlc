# Goal

## In one sentence

A complete, public, vendor-neutral reference for running software delivery with AI agents —
from spec-driven development to a software factory — organised as **AI SDLC on one page**,
where the one-page map is the front door and every part of it leads to a full reference page.

## What is being built

1. **The map is the landing page.** The five-band AI SDLC map (context, lifecycle, core,
   enablement, assurance) plus the adoption path is the home page of the site. Every label on it
   is a link, in the style of the Hopsworks documentation overview diagram.
2. **A reference page behind every box.** Each concept, phase, workflow, context variant and
   role on the map has its own complete page, written to a fixed template for its page type.
3. **Routing by purpose.** Under the map, readers choose a path by intent: *Understand*,
   *Build the kit*, *Run a workflow*, *Lead adoption*, *Look it up*.
4. **The existing series stays.** The seven-part "Beyond Faster Coding" series remains as a
   narrative introduction, cross-linked into the reference pages. The workflow catalog, glossary
   and references remain and grow.

## Who it is for

- Engineering managers and platform leads deciding how to adopt AI in delivery.
- Architects and senior engineers building the harness (the Engineering Kit).
- Product owners, QA and delivery leads whose roles change when agents do more of the work.
- Process and governance specialists (SAFe, audit, compliance) who need to see where control
  and evidence sit.

## What success looks like

- A reader can start at the map, click any box, and get a page that stands on its own, cites
  its sources and links onward.
- Every published page has passed every automated check and every reviewer, and carries a
  review record in the repository.
- The site reads as reference material: settled, present tense, complete.
- The process that produced it can be rerun, inspected and improved by anyone with the repo.

## What is out of scope

- Anything describing a specific organisation's adoption, people, products or internal numbers.
- Tool tutorials. Tools appear only as examples, or on pages specifically about a tool category.
- Opinion pieces and news. The series and the reference pages are not a blog.
